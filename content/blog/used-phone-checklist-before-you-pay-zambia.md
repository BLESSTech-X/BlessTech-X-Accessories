## Buying a Used Phone in Zambia? A Careful Checklist Before You Pay

A used phone may offer good value, but a quick look at the screen is not enough. Set aside time to test the basic functions in front of the seller and agree on the price only after you understand the device's condition.

## 1. Confirm the exact model and identity

Open the phone's Settings and check the model information. Compare it with the box or paperwork if supplied, but do not treat a box alone as proof that it belongs to the phone. Where appropriate, check the IMEI through the phone's settings or original device information and make sure the details are consistent.

Avoid buying a device if the seller cannot reasonably explain its origin or if identification details appear altered. Never publish your IMEI or other identifying device information publicly.

## 2. Check account and activation locks

Ask the seller to remove their account and turn off any activation or device-protection lock through the normal process. The phone should be reset and set up in front of you according to the manufacturer's instructions. Do not rely on a seller's promise that a lock can be bypassed later.

If the device requests the previous owner's credentials after reset, pause the purchase until the legitimate owner resolves it.

## 3. Inspect the screen and body

Look for cracks, deep scratches, dead areas, discolouration, lifted edges or signs that the screen is separating from the frame. Test touch response across the whole display using ordinary menus and typing. Inspect the frame, buttons, camera glass and charging port for damage.

A case can hide dents or cracks, so remove it with permission and check the phone itself.

## 4. Test the important functions

With the seller's permission, test:

- Touch response and display brightness.
- Front and rear cameras, focus and flash where fitted.
- Speakers, microphone and earpiece during a call or recording.
- Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and mobile signal with a compatible SIM where possible.
- Charging with a known-good compatible cable and adapter.
- Buttons, vibration, fingerprint reader or face unlock if included.

A short test cannot reveal every hidden fault, but it is better than buying without checking anything.

## 5. Ask about battery and repair history

Check the battery-health information if the phone provides it. Ask whether the battery, screen or other parts have been replaced, and whether repair receipts or warranty information are available. A seller's answer is useful context, not independent proof, so inspect the device and test it yourself.

Do not continue using or charging a phone with a swollen battery or signs of serious damage.

## 6. Protect the payment and handover

Agree on the final price and what is included—charger, cable, case, box or receipt—before paying. Test the mobile-money transaction in your own app and confirm the completed transaction yourself. Do not rely only on a screenshot or SMS shown by another person.

Keep a receipt or written record of the seller, date, model and agreed terms where possible. Meet in a sensible public place and do not let pressure or a “last chance” claim rush you.

## Before you decide

If a phone fails a key test, has an unresolved account lock, shows inconsistent identity details or the seller refuses reasonable checks, walk away. A bargain is not worth taking on a device you cannot use or confidently identify.
