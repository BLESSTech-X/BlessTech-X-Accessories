Buying a second-hand phone can save money, but a quick look at the screen is not enough. Use this checklist **before you hand over payment**. If the seller will not let you test the basics, be prepared to walk away.

## 1. Check the phone and its identity

- Compare the model shown in Settings with the model the seller advertised.
- Check the IMEI by dialling *#06# where supported, then compare it with the phone’s settings and packaging if available. A box alone is not proof of ownership.
- Ask for a receipt or written proof of sale and the seller’s contact details.
- Be cautious if identifiers are missing, altered or do not match. Do not buy a device you suspect is stolen.

## 2. Test the functions in person

Take your time and test each item yourself:

- **Screen:** check for cracks, dark spots, dead pixels, flickering and touch areas that do not respond.
- **Buttons and biometrics:** test the power and volume buttons, fingerprint reader or face unlock if available.
- **Cameras:** take a photo and short video with both front and rear cameras; check focus and flash.
- **Audio:** test the speaker, microphone and earpiece with a call or recording.
- **Connections:** test Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and mobile signal. If possible, use your own SIM to confirm calls and mobile data work.
- **Charging:** connect a compatible cable and confirm charging starts without the connector needing to be held at an awkward angle.
- **Battery:** check battery health if the model provides it. Watch for rapid percentage drops, unexpected shutdowns or unusual heat.
- **Physical condition:** inspect the charging port, frame, camera lenses and signs of liquid damage or poor repairs.

## 3. Make sure you can activate it

Ask the seller to remove their accounts and screen lock, then reset the phone in front of you where appropriate. Complete the initial setup while you are still with the seller and connected to the internet. **Do not pay for a phone that remains locked to someone else’s Apple, Google or manufacturer account.** A reset does not necessarily remove an activation lock.

Do not share your own passwords or verification codes with the seller. Enter your credentials privately only after you have decided to buy and trust the device.

## 4. Agree on the deal clearly

Confirm the final price, what accessories are included, whether any warranty or return agreement exists, and get the terms in writing. Do not rely on a promise that a fault will be fixed later. Avoid rushed meetings, suspiciously low prices and requests to send money before inspecting the device.

## 5. Remember the accessories

A used phone may come without a suitable charger or cable. Check the connector and supported charging standard for the exact model before buying replacements. A poor fit, damaged cable or unsuitable adapter can create charging problems.

### A simple rule

If you cannot test it, cannot activate it, or cannot establish a credible reason the seller has it, do not let urgency make the decision for you. A missed bargain is cheaper than paying for a phone you cannot safely or properly use.
