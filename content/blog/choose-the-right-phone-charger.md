Buying a phone charger should not feel like a guessing game. A charger that fits the port is only the first part of the decision. Here is a practical checklist to use before spending your money.

## 1. Check the connector your phone uses

Look at the charging port on your phone. Many newer Android phones use USB-C, while some older phones use Micro-USB. iPhones vary by model, with newer generations moving to USB-C and older models using Lightning. If you are unsure, check your phone model's specifications before buying.

## 2. Match the charging standard

A charger may advertise fast charging, but your phone must support the same charging technology to benefit from it. Check your phone's recommended charging standard and compare it with the charger's specifications. A higher wattage number alone does not guarantee that your phone will charge faster.

## 3. Think about power output

A compatible charger can supply more power than a phone uses, but the phone generally draws only what it supports. Still, choosing a reputable charger with clear specifications is more important than buying the biggest number on the package.

## 4. Do not ignore build quality

Look for clear branding, readable specifications, solid connectors and a cable that fits firmly. Avoid chargers with loose parts, damaged insulation, unusual heat, buzzing, or a burnt smell. Stop using a charger that shows any of these warning signs.

## 5. Check the cable too

Charging performance depends on the cable as well as the adapter. Make sure the cable supports the type of charging you want, and replace it if it is frayed, bent near the connector, or intermittently disconnects.

## The quick checklist

- Confirm the port and connector.
- Check your phone's supported charging standard.
- Compare the charger's stated output with your phone's needs.
- Choose a well-built adapter and cable.
- Stop using damaged or unusually hot equipment.
