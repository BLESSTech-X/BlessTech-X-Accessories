A charging cable can look fine on the outside and still become unreliable. Watch for these signs before a small problem turns into a daily frustration.

## 1. You have to bend it to make it work

If charging starts and stops when you move the cable, the wires or connector may be damaged. Do not keep forcing it into a position just to make it charge.

## 2. The outer covering is split or frayed

Exposed wires, deep cuts, or a split near either connector are clear reasons to stop using the cable. Tape is not a dependable repair for a damaged charging cable.

## 3. The connector feels loose

A connector that slips out easily or only works at a particular angle may be worn or damaged. Check that the phone's port is clean and undamaged too; the cable is not always the cause.

## 4. Charging or data transfer keeps failing

Repeated connection alerts, unexpectedly slow charging, or a phone that cannot transfer data may indicate a faulty cable. If possible, test with a known-good compatible cable to narrow down the cause.

## 5. The cable or connector gets unusually hot

Warmth can happen during charging, but unusual heat, a burnt smell, discoloration, or melted areas are warning signs. Disconnect it safely and do not use it again.

## Make your next cable last longer

- Pull from the connector, not the wire.
- Avoid tight bends, especially right at the ends.
- Keep cables away from liquids and high heat.
- Use the right connector and a cable suited to your device.
- Store it loosely rather than wrapping it tightly around a charger.

A braided cable can offer extra resistance to everyday scuffs, but no cable is indestructible. Choose one that matches your phone and your normal use.

**Quick reminder:** if a cable shows damage or unusual heat, replace it rather than trying to squeeze out a few more days of use. Share this checklist with someone whose cable is held together by hope and tape.
