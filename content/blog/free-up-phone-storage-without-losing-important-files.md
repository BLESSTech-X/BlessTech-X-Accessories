A “storage almost full” warning can slow down updates, stop photos from saving and make apps behave unpredictably. Before deleting anything, make a little space safely.

## 1. Find what is using the space

Open Settings and look for **Storage**. On some phones, you can also check storage inside the Files app. Look for large videos, downloads and apps you no longer use rather than deleting items at random.

## 2. Back up photos and videos first

Review your backup settings and confirm that important files have actually finished backing up before removing local copies. A photo visible in a gallery is not necessarily backed up. If you use cloud storage, check the account and backup status; if you copy files to a computer or memory card, open a few files to confirm the copy works.

## 3. Remove items you can replace

- Delete duplicate screenshots and unwanted screen recordings after checking them.
- Clear old files from Downloads that you no longer need.
- Remove offline videos, music or maps you can download again later.
- Uninstall apps you no longer use, after checking whether they contain important local data.

## 4. Clear cache carefully

Some Android apps let you clear **cache**, which usually removes temporary files. Be careful not to choose **Clear storage** or **Clear data** unless you understand the result: that can sign you out or remove information stored only on the phone. Menu names vary by device.

## 5. Check messaging apps

WhatsApp and other messaging apps can store large videos and forwarded files. Use the app’s storage-management screen to review large items and delete only what you do not need. Check important chats and documents before removing media.

## 6. Leave room for updates

Try not to keep the phone completely full. Keeping some free space helps the device save new photos, install updates and work more reliably.

**Important:** Avoid “phone cleaner” apps that promise impossible speed boosts or demand excessive permissions. Start with the storage tools already built into your phone.
