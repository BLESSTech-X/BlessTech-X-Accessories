Your phone connects you to friends, work and mobile money—but scammers may use urgency, fake support messages or stolen accounts to trick you. These checks can help you pause before acting.

## Warning signs to take seriously

- Someone says you must act immediately to avoid losing money or having your account blocked.
- A caller asks for your PIN, password, one-time password (OTP) or verification code.
- A buyer sends a screenshot as “proof of payment” and pressures you to release goods before you confirm the transaction yourself.
- A message from a friend asks for money unexpectedly, especially with a new number or unusual wording.
- A link promises a prize, job, refund or investment but asks you to sign in or provide personal details.

## Protect your WhatsApp account

1. Never share the SMS registration code or two-step verification PIN.
2. Turn on WhatsApp two-step verification and add a recovery email if available.
3. Review **Linked devices** and log out devices you do not recognise.
4. Set who can add you to groups and who can see personal profile details.
5. If a friend asks for money, verify through a separate call or another trusted channel.

## Protect mobile money transactions

- Never reveal your mobile money PIN to a caller, agent or supposed support worker.
- Read the recipient and amount carefully before confirming a transfer.
- Check your balance or transaction history through the official service—not a screenshot sent by another person.
- If money or account access may be at risk, contact your mobile network’s official support promptly and follow its account-security process.

## If you clicked a suspicious link

Do not enter more information. If you entered a password, change it through the official app or website. If you shared a payment PIN, OTP or account details, contact the relevant provider immediately. Warn contacts if your account may be compromised, and report the message using the app’s reporting tools.

## A simple rule for buying and selling online

Keep conversations and payment records, meet safely when a physical handover is needed, and confirm payment independently before handing over a product. A convincing screenshot is not proof that money has arrived.

**Remember:** legitimate support should not need you to tell them your secret PIN or verification code. When in doubt, stop and contact the provider using a number or channel you find independently.
